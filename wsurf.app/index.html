Window modes

Full sidebar, floating bar, or just the page.

Keep a reference page beside your work, then return it to the same tab without a reload.

  • Minimal window

    ⇧⌘M

    Hides the sidebar and toolbar behind one floating bar at the top or bottom — auto-hide it as you read, detach it anywhere on screen, or pin the sidebar back beside the page.

  • Micro window

    ⌥⇧⌘N

    Move a live tab into its own small window. Point at the top edge to reveal its controls. The address stays visible during sign-in prompts.

  • Float on Top

    ⌥⌘T

    Keep a Micro window above other apps, across desktops and over full-screen spaces — ideal for a layout spec, docs, or a live preview while you work.

Quiet memory

Sleep idle tabs. Keep live work awake.

Turn on Sleep inactive tabs to unload eligible pages under memory pressure or after a period of inactivity. Choose Free Memory to do it now. A sleeping tab keeps its place and reloads when you select it.

Unloaded tabs keep their pins and folders — WebKit restores Back and Forward history, while Chromium restores the address and scroll position. Even with sleeping off, critical memory pressure unloads your oldest background tabs first.

Sleeping tabs send no website notifications. Open a sleeping tab to resume browsing.

What stays awake

  • On-screen tabs Each window’s active tab and visible split panes.
  • Favorites & pins Favorites by default, plus an optional switch for pinned tabs.
  • Media & Picture in Picture Playing audio or video, and paused Picture in Picture windows.
  • Calls & capture Tabs using the camera, microphone, or screen sharing.
  • Downloads & forms Active transfers and pages with unsaved form text.
  • Private, AI & kept sites Private tabs, active assistant or MCP tabs, and sites set to stay loaded.

Opt-in iCloud sync

Shared Spaces across your Macs.

Publish Work on your first Mac and join it on your second. A tab you close on either Mac closes in that shared Space on the other.

  • Live Shared Spaces

    Publish or join a Space through iCloud so tab opens, moves, and closes stay in sync across Macs — each Mac still keeps its own active tab and local Spaces.

  • Shared pins & other devices

    Linked profiles and Spaces share pins, Favorites, and pinned folders with a cloud badge. Your Personal sidebar can also open local copies of tabs from your other Macs.

  • Encrypted vaults & selective switches

    Keep each Space’s website sign-ins separate and select the same encrypted vault on both Macs. Review duplicates before copying entries from another vault. The source stays yours to keep.

Assistant and MCP

Choose which tabs your agents can use.

Give one agent a local preview and another a documentation tab. Each connection has its own permissions. Agent Activity shows which client acted on which tab.

Built-in assistant

Ask in the address bar or hold ⌥Space to speak, using Apple Intelligence on your Mac, a local Ollama or LM Studio server, or your own cloud provider key.

Use @ to include open tabs — for example, compare two docs and fill out a long form. It never fills passwords or cards, asks before sending or purchasing, and stops the moment you click the page.

Pair Claude Code or Codex with WSurf over MCP. An agent with development access can compare a local build in WebKit and Chromium and show you where it renders differently.

Scopes, tab grants & takeover

Approve the tabs. Take over in one click.

A connection works in one regular window. You choose which pages it can read or control. Private windows stay outside its scope.

Claude Code · Development Take Over Stop

Choose Take Over on any shared tab to reclaim just that tab, or press ⌥⌘. (Stop) to revoke every connection in the window at once.

Protocol & tools in MCP.md ↗ Security boundaries ↗
  1. Read Only Approved tabs only

    Read page text, structure, and controls on the tabs you share — no clicks, typing, or navigation.

  2. Allow Control Asks on key actions

    Click, type, scroll, and fill non-sensitive fields; refuses passwords and cards and asks before purchases, sending, or signing in.

  3. Allow Development One owner per tab

    Adds viewport and device emulation, console and network diagnostics, and side-by-side WebKit vs Chromium comparison.

  4. Allow Developer Paired · Developer profile · dev sites

    Scoped to a separate Developer profile on developer sites such as localhost — adds scripts, DOM edits, and test credentials with secrets scrubbed.

Good to know

  • No separate bookmarks manager. Pins, Favorites, and folders replace it; import bookmarks from an HTML export (history is not imported).
  • Notifications. Website notifications require WSurf to be running, and sleeping tabs send no notifications.
  • Embedded Chromium. Choose Chromium per site or per tab. Extensions and native Picture in Picture use WebKit.
  • Credential Manager & passkeys. Encrypted vaults have no recovery key or master password fallback. WSurf omits Apple’s managed browser passkey entitlement, so system passkeys are not guaranteed in WebKit tabs.
  • Assistant & release status. The assistant uses AI and can make mistakes. iCloud sync and passkey unlock require the appropriate Apple signing and entitlements.
